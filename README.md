# C-47 Production Engine: downloads

C-47 Production Engine is film preproduction software: script, breakdown, shooting order, Day Out of Days, call
sheets, look book and budget, all in one project file, with every industry term explained in plain words.

This page is where the installers live. The program's source isn't published.

## Get it

Open **[Releases](../../releases)** and download the installer for your computer from the newest release:

- **Windows:** the file ending in `-setup.exe`
- **Mac:** the file ending in `.dmg` (works on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs)

It's in beta (version 0.9.1).

## Opening it the first time

The beta isn't signed by Microsoft or Apple yet, so the first time you open it, your computer warns you that it doesn't
know who made it. That's expected. You only have to get past the warning once.

### Windows

1. Double-click the `-setup.exe` file.
2. If a blue box says **Windows protected your PC**, click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. Follow the installer. The app is then in the Start menu.

### Mac

1. Double-click the `.dmg` file, then drag the app into the **Applications** folder.
2. Open **Applications** and double-click the app.
3. A box says Apple couldn't check it for malicious software. Click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
4. Open **System Settings** and choose **Privacy & Security** in the sidebar.
5. Scroll down to **Security**. Next to the line saying the app was blocked, click **Open Anyway**.
6. Click **Open Anyway** again in the box that appears, and enter your Mac password or use Touch ID if asked.

From then on it opens like any other app.

The **Open Anyway** button shows up for about an hour after you try to open the app. If you don't see it, do step 2
again, then go back to Privacy & Security.

On older Macs (macOS 14 Sonoma and earlier) there's a shortcut: right-click (or Control-click) the app in Applications,
choose **Open**, then click **Open** in the box.

## Updating

Install the new version the same way. It replaces the old one, and your project files aren't touched: they're wherever
you saved them.
